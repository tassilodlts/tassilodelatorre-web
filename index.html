About

Behind the camera.

I've had folding boats since I was twelve. Last summer I paddled a 1950s folding kayak 2,580 km down the Danube, from Ulm to the Black Sea, with my dog Fluffo as crew. I trained as an architect, I work as a model, and I've spent years in product photography. This year I'm crossing Spain on foot and by kayak, and paddling around Mallorca and Menorca.

Five languages, and a lifetime racing and paddling the coasts of southern Spain, Corsica and the Mediterranean.

What I shoot with

The kit that comes along.

Light enough to carry for months, tough enough for salt, sand and rain. Nothing here is for show; every piece earns its place on the boat.

  • Fujifilm X-T4My main camera, weather-sealed and built to keep working when the conditions do not.
  • Sigma 18-50mm f/2.8The everyday zoom, wide to short tele, for camp, coast and the people I meet.
  • Tamron 18-300mmThe reach: wildlife, far shorelines and the small details across the water.
  • DJI Mini 3 ProThe view from the air, small enough to launch from a kayak deck.
  • GoPro Hero 10 BlackIn the water, on the hull, wherever a bigger camera cannot go.

Plus a few smaller cameras for the moments the others miss.

A project to support

Eleven hundred years old, and running out of time.

High above a roadless valley in Artvin stands Nuka Sakdari, a ninth-century Georgian basilica of the Tao-Klarjeti school: its stone roof open to the sky, its walls deteriorating, never restored and scarcely documented. With a small team of architects I am making the first complete architectural record in its history, and using film to fund its emergency stabilisation. We fund, document, advocate and film, always under the authority of the Ministry of Culture. We do not touch the stones.
