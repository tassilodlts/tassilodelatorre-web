Danube · August 2025

Hold on, Europa

On the Danube, in a seventy-year-old folding boat. Written in Budapest, halfway to the Black Sea.

English Deutsche Fassung Translated from the German by the author

Standing on the roof of a Soviet boathouse, I reach into the bag, bursting with repair materials. On the far side of the Danube the parliament shines in its dignified, historic grandeur, but my eyes are on the future alone — on my seventy-year-old folding boat.

The wooden frame that holds the skin taut is broken in many places; it creaks, it wobbles. The skin leaks in countless spots, and where it was once grey it is now bright with all the patches I have given it.

“Hold on, Europa,” I whisper to the boat. “Sixty more days and we reach the delta, Ukraine, the Black Sea.” Almost a thousand kilometres we have kept the Danube company now — the folding boat and its crew, which is my black dog Fluffo and me. For a month we have been drifting along this timeless artery of Europe, from Ulm by way of Ingolstadt, Regensburg, Vienna and Bratislava to Budapest.

Four capitals, ten countries, I repeat in my head — the longest river in the European Union. And in the middle of it, between the shipping and the swell, a melancholy but optimistic son of the old continent, paddling his boat Europa, on his way to leave it for a while.

Fluffo yawns loudly. Complicated trains of thought like these mean nothing to him; he leaves them to us humans. A long slip of paper works its way out of the bag and flies off. I jump after it. It is the receipt for the makeshift repairs, for the glue. Grumpily I stuff it back. The repair material was expensive — a sunken Europe would be more expensive still, I tell myself, trying to cheer up. A bitter, ironic metaphor. My beloved Union is an expensive, heavy-hearted, patched-up boat too, in danger of going under in an ever stormier world of giants, where the rules of the powerful apply.

I'll remember that, I whisper wistfully, before glancing once more at the parliament, where Orbán's cabinet is laying its plans — and I set back to work. Because my Europe needs repairing. My future depends on it. The Danube is growing wider too, and mightier, and its waves are getting higher.

In the summer of 2025 Tassilo de la Torre Schönborn paddled 2,580 kilometres down the Danube, from Ulm to the Black Sea, in a folding boat called “Europa”, with his dog Fluffo. · tassilodelatorre.com · @tassilodlts
